The short version

Inanis Wallscape has no servers. It collects nothing about you, sends nothing to the developer or to any third party, and has no account, no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting service and no advertising identifier.

The only network traffic it produces is your device loading the websites you asked it to display, directly from those websites — exactly as if you had typed the address into a browser.

It does keep things on your device: your settings, the page it was last showing, a small diagnostic log, and a downscaled snapshot of each page so the settings screen can show you a preview. All of it lives in the app's private storage, all of it is removed when you uninstall, and none of it is ever transmitted anywhere. The next section lists every item, by file.

Inanis Wallscape never reads your photo library, contacts, location, messages, call logs, or the contents of other apps.

About the page snapshots

This is the one stored item that contains website content rather than settings, so it is worth being plain about: if a page shows a dashboard with personal information on it, a 324-pixel-wide image of that dashboard is written to the app's private storage, so the settings screen can show you a preview of each page instead of a blank tile.

Snapshots are taken only while the wallpaper is actually visible, at most once per page per minute, and never for pages set to show a picture or nothing. A page's snapshot is deleted when you change what that page shows, and snapshots for pages beyond your configured page count are removed. All of them go when you uninstall.

Websites you choose to display

When Inanis Wallscape displays a website, your device contacts that website. That website sees what it would see from any browser — your IP address, your user agent, and any cookies you already have with it. Inanis Wallscape does not add anything to those requests and does not observe them on your behalf. Those sites have their own privacy policies.

The optional per-site "Block ads and trackers" setting reduces this by refusing to load requests to a built-in list of advertising and analytics domains. It is off by default.

Precision mode (the optional accessibility service)

Inanis Wallscape includes an optional, off-by-default feature called Precision mode, which is implemented as an accessibility service named Inanis Wallscape.

Samsung doesn't tell wallpapers which home page you're on. Precision mode lets Inanis Wallscape see only the page number, nothing else. It can't read your screen, your messages, or your passwords, and nothing leaves your phone.

What exactly does it see? Your launcher already announces the page out loud for screen reader users. Precision mode reads that one short line and takes two numbers out of it:

Page 3 of 5

Inanis Wallscape keeps 3 and 5. That is the whole of it.

What happens to those two numbers. The page number is saved on your device, because that is the point of it: last_page in the app's private preferences, so a restart puts the wallpaper back on the page you were on, and a line in files/page_events.log so a page that comes back wrong can be explained. Page counts are kept the same way. Both are local to your phone and are never transmitted anywhere — see the table above for everything that is stored and where.

What it cannot access: the service is declared with canRetrieveWindowContent="false". It is therefore technically incapable of reading the content of your screen, the text you type, your passwords, which apps you open, or anything inside any app. It requests exactly two event types (typeViewSelected, typeViewScrolled) and no accessibility flags at all: no touch exploration, no gesture interception, no flagRetrieveInteractiveWindows, no key-event filtering, no ability to see other windows.

Which apps it receives events from. The manifest declares a fixed list of 13 launcher packages, and nothing else is ever in scope:

com.sec.android.app.launcher, com.google.android.apps.nexuslauncher, com.android.launcher3, app.lawnchair, com.teslacoilsw.launcher, net.oneplus.launcher, com.nothing.launcher, projekt.launcher, com.actionlauncher.playstore, com.miui.home, com.microsoft.launcher, ginlemon.flowerfree, bitpit.launcher

When the service connects it narrows that to the single package that is your current default launcher, so in normal use it receives events from one app. If your default launcher cannot be determined, the declared list above stands.

About the warning Android shows. When you turn Precision mode on, Android shows its standard warning that this app can "control your device." Android shows that same warning for every app that uses this feature. Inanis Wallscape's setting is built so it physically can't read screen content.

About a day later, Android sends a reminder asking you to review Inanis Wallscape's access. That's routine — Android does it for every app that uses this feature, and the reminder can't be turned off. Opening it or swiping it away both stop it. It comes back if Precision mode is switched off and on again, or after some app updates, so you may see it a few times early on. Keep Precision mode on to keep exact page tracking.

Control: it is off until you explicitly enable it, after a full-screen explanation inside the app. Inanis Wallscape gives you a one-tap route back to the same Android setting to turn it off again, and page detection falls back to swipe detection automatically — nothing else changes.

Inanis Wallscape declares android:isAccessibilityTool="false", because this is a non-accessibility use of the accessibility API and Android requires that to be stated.

Diagnostics, and the three things you can copy

Nothing is ever sent anywhere automatically. The Diagnostics screen has three buttons, each of which copies text to your clipboard so you can paste it into a support email if you choose to:

- Copy diagnostics — device model, Android version, launcher package, Inanis Wallscape's own settings and state, and your site names and their addresses. - Copy log — the page event log described above: page changes, swipe geometry, visibility and screen events, with timestamps. - Copy mismatch reports — the mismatch records described above, if any.

None of the three ever contains your Basic Auth credentials or any cookie. Credentials live in encrypted storage that no export reads.

The mismatch reports can be cleared from that screen. The two logs are ring buffers that overwrite themselves (500 and 400 entries) and are removed when you uninstall; there is no in-app button to clear them.

The lock screen, and the picture for when Inanis Wallscape is off

Inanis Wallscape can set a still picture you choose as your lock screen wallpaper, using Android's own wallpaper API. That writes an image to the system's lock wallpaper; it is the same thing the system wallpaper picker does, and it happens only when you ask for it. The app records the system's lock wallpaper id (lock_wallpaper_id) so it can notice when an OS update drops that picture and offer to put it back.

Likewise, when you switch Inanis Wallscape off, it can set a picture you chose as your home wallpaper so the screen is not left empty. Both features use the SET_WALLPAPER permission below and touch nothing else.

Purchases

Inanis Wallscape offers one optional one-time in-app purchase, "Pro". It is processed entirely by Google Play — Inanis Wallscape never sees or stores a payment method, and there is no account to create.

What Inanis Wallscape stores locally is a single boolean: whether Play reported the purchase as owned. It is cached on the device so that Pro features keep working at boot before Google Play Billing has connected, and while offline.

Children

Inanis Wallscape is not directed at children and collects no personal data from anyone.

Cleartext HTTP

Inanis Wallscape permits plain http:// connections because its core use case is self-hosted LAN and VPN dashboards (Home Assistant, Grafana, Pi-hole, Proxmox) which serve HTTP on private addresses and cannot hold a publicly-trusted certificate. This only ever applies to addresses you typed in yourself.

Development builds

Debug builds of Inanis Wallscape have three developer overrides — a Pro unlock, a switch that makes the app ignore launcher wallpaper offsets, and a file that fakes Advanced Protection being on. None of them is reachable in the released app, and this is verified on the shipped artifact rather than asserted: the release build's dex contains no caller for either preference, and the file name does not appear in it at all. tools/release_verify.sh re-checks this on every release.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling Inanis Wallscape removes everything listed above — preferences, encrypted credentials, WebView cookies and cache, snapshots and logs. Android deletes the app's private storage with the app. Clearing the app's storage from Android settings has the same effect without uninstalling.

A picture you chose stays wherever it already lived; Inanis Wallscape only ever held a reference to it. A lock-screen or home wallpaper you asked Inanis Wallscape to set stays set, because it belongs to the system once applied — you can change it in Android's wallpaper settings.
